Antipode

Antipode is a conversational AI system for structured dialogue between people with opposing political positions. A value-based matching system brings participants together, while a real-time orchestration layer coordinates mediation, translation, clarification and source-based claim verification throughout the conversation.

About the project

Designed and built as an end-to-end conversational system, Antipode combines matching, real-time mediation, claim verification and interaction design in one mobile application. Nothing either participant says arrives raw: each contribution passes through an orchestration layer that can reformulate charged wording, request clarification, translate across idiom and check verifiable claims against sources — attaching context where evidence is sufficient and withholding judgment where it is not. The layer's intensity is a mutual consent, not a default, and after the session every intervention is itemised as a receipt that either participant can accept or restore.

Match

Pairs participants by maximal opinion distance using a structured intake, filtered by a shared top value so the pair has somewhere to stand.

Mediate

Reformulates and contextualises, translating across idiom and culture — at a consent level either party can move, including to none.

Verify

Flags verifiable claims and provides real-time, source-backed context where the evidentiary basis is sufficient — and withholds judgment where it is not.

How you interact

Human – AI – Human

After being matched with someone holding substantially different views, participants speak to each other through a real-time mediation layer. Each contribution is transcribed and assessed before it reaches the other person: it can be translated, neutrally reformulated, followed up for clarification or checked against external sources. The participants remain in direct dialogue with each other, while the AI intervenes only where the conversation requires mediation or context.

System architecture

Antipode is built as a modular mediation system that combines voice-first interaction, orchestration, real-time evidence verification and persistent conversational state within a deployable single-file application. Voice and text input move through a shared pipeline that coordinates mediation, verification and context management before assembling a response; profiles, consent and conversation state are stored separately from the generative layer.

What it investigates

When AI mediates a conversation, it also shapes the conditions under which that conversation takes place.

Antipode investigates how mediation changes political dialogue: what happens when emotionally charged language is reformulated, vague arguments trigger follow-up questions, claims are checked against external sources and insufficient evidence leads the system to withhold judgment. These interventions can reduce immediate friction, but they also introduce new forms of influence through wording, timing, source selection and evidence thresholds. The project therefore treats neutrality as a system-design problem: when should AI intervene, what should remain untouched, and how can those interventions stay visible and contestable to the people in the conversation?

Where it applies

Matching, mediation, translation, verification and reflection are implemented as separate system functions. By adapting conversation rules, source context and interaction setup, Antipode can support different dialogue formats without changing the underlying mediation logic.

Civic dialogue

Structured conversations around contested public issues, participation processes and deliberative formats.

Cross-language dialogue

Real-time translation and mediation for conversations where language differences would otherwise add friction or limit participation.

Facilitated dialogue

Moderated formats for workshops, research and public participation, with clarification, source context and structured reflection integrated into the conversation.

What it led to

Public testing informed the next iteration of the system. The pilot surfaced the two changes that mattered most: participants wanted the mediation level to be a visible, shared control rather than a setting, and they wanted every intervention recoverable after the fact. Both are now core — the consent dial in the conversation header, and the receipts screen where any intervention can be restored to its original wording.
